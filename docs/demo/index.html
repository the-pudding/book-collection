This is 5,000 menus from the New York Public Library’s Buttolph Collection, an archive of over 25,000 menus mostly ranging from 1880-1920. The collection shouldn’t exist, but it does, due to the determination of one woman.

From 1900-1925, Frank Buttolph was determined to archive every menu in the world. For 25 years, she would visit restaurants to collect menus. She took out ads in newspapers, requesting owners mail prestine copies. If a restaurant wouldn’t share a copy, she’d write to their menu printers. She then moved onto archiving menus from private banquets and events. After that, she started on menus from trains. And then Transatlantic ships. And then from cities in Europe and Asia.

Buttolph said, “[My] work has been the only thing I had to live for. It was my heart, my soul, my life.”

But not everyone saw this work as important, once described as a “wasted effort.” Back then, menus and restaurants weren’t the same as they are now.
